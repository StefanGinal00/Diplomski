# World structure (working plan)

The game is a connected 2D world, not a level-select sequence. A **zone** is a large region with a shared visual identity; each main zone has enemies and a boss, while Starfall Citadel is a fully peaceful city **inside** the fourth and final region. A **room** is a smaller place connected by doors, shafts or shortcuts. Starfall's safe city is an intentional exception: its districts share one continuous scene. Players can revisit every unlocked route; defeating a boss opens the next zone but does not close the previous one.

The Training Passage and Void Sentinel are the prologue. Four main zones are the current target for the finished game. Room names beyond the currently playable route are working names, not claims that the content already exists.

## Route rules

- Boss kills and unique keys are persistent **only after saving at a lamp**. On death, Normal mode restores the last lamp snapshot; Hardcore keeps its existing one-run rule.
- Once a boss or mini-boss encounter starts, room doors, level exits, lamp resting and fast travel seal until victory or death. This also applies to the Cinder Coliseum's full four-wave trial, not just its final Marshal wave. The world map can still be viewed, and normal backtracking returns after victory; quitting or loading follows the established lamp snapshot and mode rules.
- First-time boss fights begin on approach. Awakened Warden, Matriarch and Castellan rematches are optional and display **ATTACK TO CHALLENGE**; merely passing them while backtracking does not start a fight or seal the route. A hit begins the rematch and commits the player to the same arena lock.
- Forward gates may require boss defeats, key items and specific world events together. Return doors never require a boss a second time. Shortcuts should create loops, not one-way traps.
- Crossing a room door now requires an explicit action: approach to reveal its prompt, then press [E] or click/tap the nearby door. Merely touching it never changes rooms, and a locked door reports its missing requirement only when the player tries it. This applies to forward gates, side routes and return shortcuts; arrival markers remain clear of the opposing door.
- The Hollow Throne gate requires the three prior main bosses **and three Memory Sigils**: Shaft from Blackwater Cistern's upper reliquary after the pump, Echo from Prism Archive's mirror sequence, and Ash from Ashen Chapel's side reliquary after the bells. These are key items and cannot be bought or dropped. The Starfall Guardian stands beyond the safe city boundary, but its fight is avoidable via Empty Court's upper spur and is not a final-gate prerequisite. The sigils make the optional earlier discoveries relevant on the final route without closing backtracking.
- Each sigil now reveals a short non-pausing memory on discovery. Shaft's drowned bell, Echo's mirror voices and Ash's saved ember also remain readable in their inventory descriptions. Shaft and Ash reliquaries have distinct rotating rune halos and colors; Echo keeps its mirror puzzle. Finding all three adds a brief reminder that the final gate also needs the three earlier guardian defeats. Discovery cards are transient UI, not another saved flag: collected sigils remain in the lamp snapshot and their cards do not replay on reload.
- Each combat zone should grow toward seven or eight distinct adventure rooms (a safe settlement may be an additional stop), with at least one hub, one optional branch, one traversal challenge, a lamp before its boss, and a return shortcut. The count is not a reason to make eight identical corridors. Starfall is deliberately different: its districts are physically connected streets and upper paths in one safe city, without room-transition doors between neighborhoods.
- The safe settlements in Echo and Ash are connected subareas, not enemy-free replacements for those combat zones. From the Echo Grotto and Broken Causeway, the player first crosses a dangerous outer road with enemies, then passes through a visible gate into Whisperlight Haven or Cinder Hearth. The town name appears on entering the safe core, not on reaching its approach. Both directions remain open; enemies, traps and awakened reinforcements never spawn inside the safe core. Starfall is the larger exception: a continuous peaceful city core, with the fourth zone's future danger only beyond a distinct outer boundary.
- Pacing target for the expanded game: a player's **first meaningful visit through each ordinary adventure room** should last roughly 5-10 minutes, excluding menus and idle time. This is **per room, not per zone**. Multiple climbs, descents, encounters, optional branches and a clear return route should provide that time; a larger rectangle alone does not prove the target. Boss arenas are intentionally more compact. Revisit and fast-travel routes should be quicker. Stopwatch-based hands-on playtesting is still required before claiming that any room meets the target.
- Save Lamps share one fast-travel network. A lamp becomes a destination after resting and saving there; previously discovered lamps remain lit and usable after loading. Travel starts at a discovered lamp with [T], while [M] only views the map when away from a lamp. Arriving at a lamp saves and makes it the new respawn point.
- All four combat zones now have a connected first-pass boss route. After the Castellan, Starfall is one continuous safe city from the gate through Lantern Ward, Market and Celestial Garden, with upper walkways and a reversible return door. Its far-edge gate leads through Outer Watch, Silent Gate, Memory Vault and Rooted Hall to Empty Court, then along an upper spur to Soul Crucible, Sunless Passage and the Hollow Throne. The Guardian's lower arena remains optional. Defeating the Hollow Sovereign opens a short working-draft epilogue, then allows continued exploration. There is no door to unbuilt content or credits sequence yet.

## Currently playable

- Four expedition detours remain on the **first-visit forward route**: Wisp Hollow → Driftworks → Drowned Crossing, Whispering Gallery → Resonant Depths → Prism Archive, Broken Causeway → Emberspine → Cinder Forge, and Outer Watch → Broken Ramparts → Silent Gate. Their original direct links become shortcuts on revisits. Expedition danger rooms use an explicit chamber graph: eight broad rooms with continuous ground, reversible shafts at different X positions, four optional side chambers and one alternate loop. A route must include both ascents and descents. Small jump platforms are reserved for shafts, ruined bridges and deliberate traversal challenges; they must never be used as the floor of every room. Boss arenas remain deliberately shorter. Dimensions are not measured completion times.
- Expanded routes distribute hostiles, neutral creatures, flora, caches and sometimes-empty breakable crates across their full length, with side shafts or niches and return shortcuts. Boss arenas remain intentionally shorter. Inactive rooms stop processing and rendering at runtime, though mobile memory and frame-rate still need profiling. Enemy placement, rewards and the 5–10 minute goal require hands-on balancing.
- **Room streaming pass (stateful traversal unload implemented):** the activity director hides and fully pauses inactive rooms. The global curated population, all large Shaft/Echo/Ash/Starfall traversal extensions, the four expedition wings, the added Echo/Cinder districts and the ambient upper-town residents keep persistent terrain, doors, lamps, puzzle state and lightweight scenery in the initial world but instantiate each room's designated dynamic portion only on first entry. All ordinary expanded Shaft, Echo, Ashen and Starfall routes moved their original entry-court enemies, neutral creatures and breakables out of the `.tscn` roots into explicit authored manifests. Both those manifests and the four biomes' generated traversal combatants, fauna and breakables now unload ten seconds after the player leaves. Awakened Shaft/Echo/Ash patrols also spawn only in the active room, use late-route anchors from the expanded layouts, and share the same stateful unload/restore rules; their reward caches now sit inside optional expanded branches instead of retaining obsolete small-map coordinates. A per-room/key registry restores surviving actors with exact health, local position and neutral hostility/rest state; defeated enemies and destroyed crates remain absent for that run. Persistent caches, hazards, shortcut controls and one-shot localized encounter controllers stay loaded so progression cannot reset. Echo Nest's veil explicitly reconnects to its streamed brood and only counts broodlings inside that room. Re-entry never duplicates population, and UI/quest signal wiring accepts reconstructed enemies and NPCs. Hand-authored actors in settlement cores/boss rooms and expedition-wing controllers remain deliberately persistent until profiling shows they need a separate safe lifecycle. Never unload an active boss arena, dialogue, save interaction or transition target. Pool high-frequency projectiles/FX, preload the next connected room asynchronously, and profile memory/frame time on the target phone before choosing final adjacent-room and settlement budgets.
- Room identity is now mechanical as well as visual. Shaft rooms distinguish mining rockfalls, non-lethal water currents, valve-controlled pressure jets, pump chambers, sentry sightlines and the broken hoist. Echo distinguishes crystal choirs, listening chambers, mirror/refraction stacks, marked currents, brood nurseries, phased bridge spurs and seal-controlled undertow passages. Ash distinguishes forge heat lanes, barracks drills, reservoir surges, chapel relics and siege supplies. Starfall distinguishes warded gates, memory monoliths, living roots, Crucible vats and Sunless surge lanes. Telegraphs and safe alternatives remain part of every mandatory first-pass route.
- The opening Training Passage now reads as three small landmarks—a Wayfarer camp, old aqueduct and Sentinel court—without moving its checkpoint, quest nodes or exit. A screen-space parallax backdrop gives Training, Shaft, Echo, Ash and Starfall different palettes, depth silhouettes and region-specific motes; safe settlements use warmer variants. It is decorative only and ignores pointer input.
- For visual review in Godot, open an individual room `.tscn` (for example `VerticalChamber.tscn` or `StarfallOutskirts.tscn`) to inspect full collision, decoration and encounters. `Game.tscn` deliberately uses lightweight outlines for the largest `@tool` expansions: each outlined rectangle is a traversable chamber and each narrow connector is a shaft/tunnel. It is a technical overview, not the final player map. The in-game map should separately present the whole world as an irregular two-dimensional network of districts, rooms, dead ends, locked links and shortcuts.

Cinder Hearth now extends its street to the copper archive and upper watch. Starfall Citadel is already a large continuous safe city with rooftop and civic paths. All settlements still need a later final-art, dialogue and interaction pass.

Fiends gain health and speed, Sentries gain health and wider volleys, and the heat vents cycle faster. The map records visited rooms and shows Echo-room, cache and rematch progress beside the lamp list.

The ordinary danger rooms now have substantially larger first-pass layouts; the bosses remain more compact. The larger footprints do not yet establish a 5–10-minute playtime: route clarity, enemy density, art, animation, sound and on-device performance require measured playtests and iteration before production completion.

## Peaceful settlements and services

- Whisperlight Haven is reached from the Echo Grotto through a dangerous crystal road; Cinder Hearth is reached from Broken Causeway through an ash-covered outer road. Each approach has two enemies and a visible gate. Passing the gate displays the settlement name and changes the objective and ambience to the safe core; crossing back shows danger again. The safe cores have open return doors, a Save Lamp linked to fast travel, residents, and no enemies or hazards. Awakened-zone upgrades do not populate the cores with threats.
- Lyra now gives and receives the existing Echo Survey in Whisperlight Haven. Mira in Cinder Hearth offers a one-time task to activate the Cinder Forge cooling fan (2 XP, 50 Gold and 2 Iron Fragments). Tarin watches the town gate and asks the player to clear its dangerous outer road; the two specific targets award 1 XP, 25 Gold and an Iron Fragment on return. Progress and rewards follow lamp-save rollback rules. These quests demonstrate that a town can send the player both directly beyond its gate and farther across its region.
- Each settlement has a local trader and an anvil. Anvils reuse the five-rank weapon forge and its material costs. Local traders have a small fixed stock of herbs, one Life Bloom and selected ammunition/materials; each displayed remaining count is enforced by the purchase operation and included in save data. Stock is per journey, not refreshed by merely re-entering or resting. Orin's existing shop and discount remain separate.
- Non-quest residents now follow short marked street routes. They pause at meeting places, trade brief overhead lines with another resident, and fade through visible house or shop doors before returning to the street. They stop moving when a player starts a dialogue. These are deliberately lightweight ambient cycles, not saved progression; loading a lamp can reset where a resident is on their route.
- These are first playable settlement slices, not final city art or size. House entry is currently a brief off-screen interval behind a doorway, not an explorable interior. The gates are simple geometric landmarks for now. Later map expansion can add actual interiors, longer outer districts, streets, residents and quest chains without trapping players or making the safe cores spawn enemies during awakened revisits.
- The outer roads also contain neutral creatures: a Mossling wanders and rests near Whisperlight, and an Ash Grazer sleeps outside Cinder Hearth. They neither chase nor damage the player on sight, but a hit wakes them with a brief visible warning before they fight back. They are optional; killing them does not advance mandatory enemy-clear quests or Tarin's gate task. The current sprites are placeholders for later creature art and animation.

## City and depth art pass (planned, not implemented)

Replace the present geometry with readable silhouettes first, then add lighting and detail; keep interactable doors, NPCs and hazards distinct from decoration.
- Build layered parallax backgrounds for each region: slow distant silhouettes, a mid-depth layer with architecture/foliage, and faster near details, plus restrained foreground occlusion and atmospheric color. Aim for the sense of depth in hand-painted side-scrollers without copying another game's assets or layouts. Test camera movement, legibility of platforms and mobile performance before increasing layer count or particle effects.
- Starfall Citadel has a first-pass continuous peaceful city with several streets and upper routes. Deepen it with more distinct house/shop silhouettes, enterable interiors where useful, fuller daily NPC schedules, story quests, changing dialogue at timeline milestones and reasons to revisit. The Guardian encounter is already outside the city wall; keep the city itself free of hostile spawns. The smaller havens in Echo and Ash remain safe pockets within otherwise dangerous zones.

These are lightweight geometry placeholders for the later art pass and do not add collision or hostile spawns.

## Later expansion passes (planned, not implemented)

- After the basic routes through all main zones work, add a small number of optional rooms or subroutes that open **only after the first clear** of their zone. The first boss kill should visibly reveal the route; the new content must remain reversible, scale its rewards with awakened difficulty and never block the original main path. Avoid simply hiding a mandatory story room behind a repeat clear.
- Redesign the Skills menu as a navigable multi-branch tree. Core movement/survival, Sword, Bow, Staff/Magic and Defense are useful **examples**, not a fixed requirement of exactly five branches; merge or add branches according to the final skill roster. A branch-selection view opens its own node graph, with clear prerequisites, costs, acquired-state feedback and controller/touch-friendly navigation. Existing learned skills and saved points must migrate without loss; the current compact menu remains until this redesign is ready.
- Give each main boss a recognizable set of roughly two or three core attacks plus a larger pool of about six or seven special moves across phases and rematches. Do not fire the whole pool at once: phase and difficulty select readable subsets with clear warnings, recovery windows and counters for melee, bow and magic. ## Forge and build economy

- Orin's shop now has Buy and Forge tabs. Iron Fragments can be purchased as a fallback or found in caches, crates and occasional Shaft Crawler drops; Ether Dust remains available from wisps, caches and the shop.
- The Basic Sword, Spiritglass Blade, Hunter Bow, Thorn Bow, Runed Staff and Sunder Staff each have five permanent forge ranks. +1, +3 and +5 each add 1 damage to that weapon's attacks, including special arrows or both staff spells. +2 shortens attack cooldown by 12%; +4 brings the total reduction to 18%. +5 also gives the equipped weapon a pulsing radiance. Each rank consumes gold and weapon-appropriate materials; Orin's earned discount applies to the gold portion.
- +4 and +5 need Resonance Shards from the awakened Echo Grotto. The first Matriarch victory, her rematch and two upgraded-room caches provide them; Orin sells more after the first victory so upgrading several weapons never depends on a finite drop.
- Upgrade ranks are stored separately from inventory, so changing the equipped weapon does not erase a build. As with other progression, a lamp save is needed before a Normal-mode death, and Hardcore death deletes the run.
- The defense slot now has two mutually exclusive shop choices: Guardian Band reduces hits of at least 2 damage by 1 (never below 1), while Wind Cloak shortens Dash cooldown by 25%. Buying either equips it; the inventory can switch or unequip them. Owned defense gear and the active choice follow the same lamp-save rules as weapons.

## Enemy families and weapon identity

- Current enemies carry one family tag: Spirit (wisps, shades, Matriarch), Demon (passage fiends, Warden), Beast (crawlers, broodlings) or Construct (sentries, passage guardian). Boss is a separate additive tag. These assignments are gameplay scaffolding and can be refined alongside the final creature designs.
- After the Abyss Warden falls, Orin sells three specialized alternatives. Spiritglass Blade reaches farther but swings more slowly than the Basic Sword and deals +1 to Spirits. Thorn Bow fires faster but has less range than Hunter Bow and deals +1 to Beasts. Sunder Staff has heavier, slower, shorter-range spells than Runed Staff and deals +1 to Constructs. A matching boss receives the family bonus because Boss is an additional tag.
- Bow and staff projectile damage and travel distance now come from the equipped weapon's stats. Ember Arrows and Frost Orb retain their own ammo/spell behavior, and family bonuses apply on impact. Class skills, ammunition and spell controls work with either weapon of that class. All six weapons have independent forge ranks and follow the same lamp-save rules.

## Weapon skill branches

- Each weapon class has a second skill costing 1 Skill Point and requiring its first Mastery. Long Reach extends sword hitbox range by 16. Piercing Shot lets basic arrows hit two distinct enemies, while Ember Arrows remain single-target. Mana Flow gives 50% faster mana regeneration while any staff is the active weapon.
- These passives require no extra combat controls. The skill panel groups each second skill below its prerequisite, and inventory/HUD labels reflect the relevant bonuses. Unlocks are included in lamp saves and roll back on Normal-mode death if not saved.
